Coordinate geometry

Investigate ordered pairs, axes, the origin and the four quadrants by plotting points on a Cartesian plane.

Investigation 01 / Plot a point

Enter an ordered pair (x, y). The point is plotted on the Cartesian plane and its location is identified.

Point (3, 2) is in Quadrant I.
The horizontal number is always written first: (x, y). Points on either axis are not inside a quadrant.

Investigation 02 / Slope, y-intercept and straight-line graph

Enter a straight-line equation such as y = 2x + 3 or x = 2y + 3. MathLab rearranges it into y = mx + c, identifies slope and y-intercept, and plots the line.

—Slope (m)
—Y-intercept (c)
—Rearranged form
Enter a line equation to analyse it.
For y = mx + c, m is the slope and c is the y-intercept. A vertical line x = k has an undefined slope and no single y-intercept; this tool reports that separately.

Investigation 03 / Distance and midpoint

Compare two points on the coordinate plane. MathLab calculates the straight-line distance and the midpoint between them.

—Distance AB
—Midpoint M
Distance and midpoint steps will appear here.
Distance = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]. Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

Investigation 05 / Experimental and theoretical probability

Experimental probability is based on what happens in trials. Theoretical probability is calculated from equally likely outcomes before an experiment.

P(E) = favourable outcomes ÷ total equally likely outcomes

After a simulation, compare the observed fraction with the theoretical fraction. They may differ in a small experiment; more trials often give a more stable estimate, but do not guarantee an exact match.
